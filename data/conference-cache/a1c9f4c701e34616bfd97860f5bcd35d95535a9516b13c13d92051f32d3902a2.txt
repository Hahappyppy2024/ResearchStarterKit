	<h2>
		<a class="title" href="https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/issue/view/551">
							AAAI-23 Technical Tracks 4
					</a>
					<div class="series">
				Vol. 37 No. 4
			</div>
			</h2>

	<div class="description">
		<p><strong>Thirty-Seventh AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence</strong><br><strong>Thirty-Fifth Conference on Innovative Applications of Artificial Intelligence</strong><br><strong>Thirteenth Symposium on Educational Advances in Artificial Intelligence</strong><br><strong> Edited by Brian Williams, Yiling Chen, Jennifer Neville </strong><br><em>Sponsored by the Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence</em><br>February 7–14, 2023, Washington DC, USA.</p>
<p>Published by AAAI Press, Washington, DC, USA<br>Copyright © 2023, Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence<br>1101 Pennsylvania Ave, NW, Suite 300, Washington, DC 20004<br>All Rights Reserved<br>ISSN 2374-3468 (Online) <br>ISSN 2159-5399 (Print) <br>ISBN 978-1-57735-880-0 (Online, 13 issue set)</p>
<p>The Thirty-Seventh AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence was held on February 7–14, 2023 in Washington, D.C., USA. The program chairs were Yiling Chen (Harvard University, USA) and Jennifer Neville (Microsoft Research and Purdue University, USA).</p>
<p>The AAAI-23 welcomed submissions on research that advances artificial intelligence, broadly conceived. The conference featured technical paper presentations, special tracks, invited speakers, workshops, tutorials, poster sessions, senior member presentations, competitions, exhibit programs, and two new activities: a Bridge Program and a Lab Program. Many of these activities were tailored to the theme of bridges and were selected according to the highest standards, with additional programs for students and young researchers. The conference scope included machine learning (deep learning, statistical learning, etc), natural language processing, computer vision, data mining, multiagent systems, knowledge representation, human-in-the-loop AI, search, planning, reasoning, robotics and perception, and ethics. In addition to fundamental work that focused on any one of these areas, AAAI-23 encouraged work across technical areas of AI, (e.g., machine learning and computer vision; computer vision and natural language processing; or machine learning and planning), bridges between AI and a related research area (e.g., neuroscience; cognitive science) or developing AI techniques in the context of important application domains, such as healthcare, sustainability, transportation, and commerce.</p>
<p>The conference also continued its tradition of colocating with the long-running Innovative Applications of Artificial Intelligence conference (cochaired by Alex Wong, University of Waterloo, Canada, and YuHao Chen, University of Waterloo, Canada). The IAAI papers are included in this proceedings. Also included are the papers from the Symposium on Educational Advances in Artificial Intelligence (cochaired by Marion Neumann, Washington University, USA, Pat Virtue, Carnegie Mellon University, USA, and Michael Guerzhoy, University of Toronto, Canada).</p>
<p>The proceedings have been published in 13 consecutive issues. This issue (volume 37 no. 4) consists of 1,557 pages and three track:</p>
<p>AAAI Technical Track on Constraint Satisfaction and Optimization<br>AAAI Technical Track on Data Mining &amp; Knowledge Management<br>AAAI Technical Track on Domain(s) of Application</p>
