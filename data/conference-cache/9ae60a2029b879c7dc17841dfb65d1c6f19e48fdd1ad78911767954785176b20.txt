	<h2>
		<a class="title" href="https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/issue/view/651">
							IAAI-25, EAAI-25, AAAI-25 Student Abstracts, Undergraduate Consortium and Demonstrations
					</a>
					<div class="series">
				Vol. 39 No. 28
			</div>
			</h2>

	<div class="description">
		<p><strong>Thirty-Ninth AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence</strong><br><strong>Thirty-Seventh Conference on Innovative Applications of Artificial Intelligence</strong><br><strong>Fifteenth Symposium on Educational Advances in Artificial Intelligence</strong><br><strong> Edited by Toby Walsh, Julie Shah, Zico Kolter </strong><br><em>Sponsored by the Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence</em><br>February 25–March 4, 2025, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.</p>
<p>Published by AAAI Press, Washington, DC, USA<br>Copyright © 2025, Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence<br>601 Pennsylvania Ave, NW, Suite 900, Washington, DC 20004<br>All Rights Reserved<br>ISSN 2374-3468 (Online) <br>ISSN 2159-5399 (Print) <br>ISBN-10: 1-57735-897-X <br>ISBN-13: 978-1-57735-897-8</p>
<p>The Thirty-Ninth AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence was held on February 25 – March 4, 2025, Philadelphia, Pennyslvania. The program chairs were Julie Shah (Massachusetts Institute of Technology, USA) and Zico Kolter (Carnegie Mellon University, USA).</p>
<p>AAAI-25 welcomed submissions on research that advances artificial intelligence, broadly conceived. The conference featured technical paper presentations, special tracks, invited speakers, workshops, tutorials, poster sessions, senior member presentations, competitions, and exhibit programs. Many of these activities were tailored to the theme of bridges and were selected according to the highest standards, with additional programs for students and young researchers. In addition to the Main Technical Track, authors were encouraged to submit papers for the Special Track on AI for Social Impact and the Special Track on AI Alignment.</p>
<p>Driven by its disciplinary diversity, AAAI has incubated numerous AI sub-disciplines and conferences and has nurtured for decades the cohesion of AI. The purpose of this year’s Bridge Program is to tap into new sources of innovation by cultivating collaboration between two or more communities directed towards a common goal. Hence, the communities that our Bridge Program is intended to bring together could be distinct subfields of AI, such as planning and learning, or different disciplines that contribute to and benefit from AI, such as AI and the humanities.</p>
<p>The conference scope included machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, data mining, multiagent systems, knowledge representation, human-in-the-loop AI, search, planning, reasoning, robotics and perception, and ethics. In addition to fundamental work that focused on any one of these areas, AAAI-25 encouraged work across technical areas of AI, (e.g., machine learning and computer vision; computer vision and natural language processing; or machine learning and planning), bridges between AI and a related research area (e.g., neuroscience; cognitive science) or developing AI techniques in the context of important application domains, such as healthcare, sustainability, transportation, and commerce.</p>
<p>The conference also continued its tradition of collocating with the long-running Innovative Applications of Artificial Intelligence conference (IAAI-25). IAAI-25 was cochaired by Jan Seyler (Festo, Germany), Serdar Kadioglu (Brown University, USA) and Sean McGregor (UL Research Institutes, USA). The IAAI-25 papers are included in this proceedings. Also included are the papers from the Symposium on Educational Advances in Artificial Intelligence (EAAI-25). EAAI-25 was cochaired by Stephanie Rosenthal (Carnegie Mellon University, USA) and Narges Norouzi (University of California Berkley, USA)</p>
<p>The proceedings have been published in 28 consecutive issues. This issue (volume 39 no. 28) consists of 991 pages and 13 tracks:</p>
<p>IAAI Technical Track on Deployed Highly Innovative Applications of AI<br>IAAI Technical Track on Emerging Applications of AI<br>IAAI Technical Track on Deployed Innovative Tools for Enabling AI Applications<br>IAAI Technical Track on Innovative Inter-disciplinary AI Integration<br>IAAI Technical Track on AI Incidents and Best Practices<br>EAAI Symposium: Main Track<br>EAAI Symposium: Resources for Teaching AI in K-12<br>EAAI Symposium: Mentored Undergraduate Research Challenge<br>EAAI Symposium: Model AI Assignments<br>AAAI Doctoral Consortium Track<br>AAAI Student Abstract and Poster Program<br>AAAI Undergraduate Consortium<br>AAAI Demonstration Track</p>

The conference featured technical paper presentations, special tracks, invited speakers, workshops, tutorials, poster sessions, senior member presentations, competitions, and exhibit programs. Many of these activities were tailored to the theme of bridges and were selected according to the highest standards, with additional programs for students and young researchers. The purpose of this year’s Bridge Program was to tap into new sources of innovation by cultivating collaboration between two or more communities directed towards a common goal including distinct subfields of AI, such as planning and learning, or different disciplines that contribute to and benefit from AI, such as AI and the humanities.</p>
<p>The conference scope included machine learning (deep learning, statistical learning, etc), natural language processing, computer vision, data mining, multiagent systems, knowledge representation, human-in-the-loop AI, search, planning, reasoning, robotics and perception, and ethics. In addition to fundamental work that focused on any one of these areas, AAAI-24 encouraged work across technical areas of AI, (e.g., machine learning and computer vision; computer vision and natural language processing; or machine learning and planning), bridges between AI and a related research area (e.g., neuroscience; cognitive science) or developing AI techniques in the context of important application domains, such as healthcare, sustainability, transportation, and commerce.</p>
<p>The conference also continued its tradition of colocating with the long-running Innovative Applications of Artificial Intelligence conference (IAAI-24). IAAI-24 was cochaired by Alex Wong (University of Waterloo, Canada), YuHao Chen (University of Waterloo, Canada) and Jan R. Seyler (Festo, Germany). The IAAI-24 papers are included in this proceedings. Also included are the papers from the Symposium on Educational Advances in Artificial Intelligence (EAAI-24). EAAI-24 was cochaired by Marion Neumann (Washington University, USA) and Stephanie Rosenthal (Carnegie Mellon University, Pittsburgh, USA)</p>
<p>The proceedings have been published in 21 consecutive issues. This issue (volume 38 no. 20) consists of 836 pages and 6 tracks:</p>
